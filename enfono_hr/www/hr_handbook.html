02 The reports

Every report shares the same four filters — Employee, Branch, Department, Designation. Leave them blank to see everything you have access to. Any report can be exported to Excel or PDF from the … menu at the top right.

Branch scoping

If you are not an HR Manager or System Manager, reports show only your own branch unless you pick a different one. HR Managers see all branches by default.

Names are blurred in this guide only

The screenshots below have employee names, IDs and pay columns deliberately obscured, because this guide's images are readable by anyone who can reach the address. On the real screens everything is fully visible to whoever has permission.

Daily Leave Request Report

Leave requests as they were submitted. Reported Date & Time is when the request landed in the tray — not the date being asked for. Defaults to today.

Late Entry Detail Report

Anyone who arrived more than the grace period after their shift start.

  • Grace Period (Minutes) filter — defaults to 15. Change it to see what a different rule would catch, without changing the rule itself.
  • Working Hours column — added on purpose. If a row shows a huge lateness but almost no working hours, that is a check-in app mis-tap, not a real day. Check it before acting on it.

Early Exit Detail Report

The mirror image, measured against shift end. Overnight shifts (8PM–8AM) are handled correctly. Check-outs created automatically at 23:00 by the system are excluded — those are not real early exits.

Previous Day Checkout Not Marked Report

Defaults to yesterday, which is how you should run it — first thing each morning.

ReasonMeaning
No Check-out LoggedNothing was recorded at all
Auto Check-Out AppliedThe system stamped a 23:00 check-out to stop them being marked Absent

Both mean the employee did not check out. The Half Day Requested column shows whether they filed a half-day leave — which is the difference between a penalty and no penalty.

Monthly Attendance Sheet Detail

One row per employee, one column per day.

P Present · A Absent · H Half Day · L On Leave · W Work From Home · O Holiday · blank = nothing marked

A blank cell is worth looking at: unmarked days are treated as Absent by payroll.

Leave Balance Report

Opening balance, allocated, carried forward, taken, expired, closing — per employee per leave type. Defaults to the current year.

This uses the same calculation as ERPNext’s own leave balance, so it will always agree with what an employee sees. It replaces the four older balance reports.

Break Marking Report

Breaks are worked out from check-out / check-in pairs during the day — the system has no separate “break” button.

  • Minimum Break (Minutes) — ignores accidental double taps. Default 1.
  • Maximum Break (Minutes) — default 240. Someone who checks out at 09:00 and back in at 18:00 did not take a nine-hour break; that is absence.

Limitation worth knowing

An employee who never logs out for lunch shows no break. This report reflects logging discipline, not actual behaviour.

Tomorrow Leave List

Who is off tomorrow, grouped by branch. Tick Include Pending Approval to also see requests that have not been approved yet.

Advance Payment Request Report

All advances with their outstanding balance. The Approval Route column shows Standard or Driver & Marketing — set automatically from the employee’s designation.

03 Enfono HR Settings — where the rules live

Ctrl + G → Enfono HR Settings

The habit to build

This page decides what everyone gets paid. After any change, run the Payroll Computation Preview for a full month and check the totals before the next payroll run. There is a button on the settings page that takes you straight there.

Attendance Penalties

These are charged on top of the pay ERPNext already deducts for an absent or half day. Each of the three kinds can be set three ways: Days of Salary, Fixed Amount, or None.

Read this before setting the missing-checkout penalty

In July 2026, 51 of 56 employees missed at least one check-out. It is a logging habit, not 51 disciplinary cases.

Charging one full day per occurrence removes about a third of total payroll and takes two employees to zero pay. Measured on real data:

Free Missing Checkouts Per Month gives an allowance before anything is charged — the gentlest way to make the rule corrective rather than punitive.

Safety Limits

These stop any rule taking more than a month can pay. Anything they hold back is shown on the preview report, never silently dropped.

Overtime

Hourly rate = Base ÷ 31 ÷ 8. Both divisors are editable, but 31 and 8 is the agreed basis — a 12-hour driver and an 8-hour office worker earn the same hourly OT rate.

Pay an Extra Day for Working the Weekly Off adds one day’s salary when someone works their weekly off (Sunday by default). This is separate from OT hours, so the two never double-count.

Daily Wage Defaults

₹300 per day, ₹400 on the weekly off. Used for employees whose Wage Type is Daily Wage and who have no rate of their own.

04 Daily-wage employees

On the Employee form, in the Wage Type section:

  1. Set Wage Type to Daily Wage
  2. Set Daily Wage Rate and Weekly-Off Wage Rate (or leave blank to use the settings defaults)

Daily-wage employees are paid per day worked and receive no casual leave.

Not yet done

All employees currently default to Monthly. The Chelambra factory daily-wagers must be set to Daily Wage, or they will be paid as salaried staff.

05 The three new forms

Late Entry Early Exit Request

Use this to excuse a late arrival or early departure. An approved request replaces the flat ₹100 fine for that day with an hourly deduction instead.

  1. New → pick Employee, Date, Request Type
  2. Enter the Reason (required)
  3. Save — the system reads the real minutes from that day’s attendance
  4. Set Status to Approved or Rejected, then Submit

The minutes are re-read from attendance every time you save. You cannot excuse more time than the employee actually missed.

Employee Arrear

Money the employee owes, recovered over several months.

FieldMeaning
Outstanding AmountTotal to recover
Monthly Deduction AmountTaken from each payslip
Deduction Start / End MonthAny date inside the first / last month
Additional Deduction AmountOne-off extra, taken in the first month only
Comments / RemarksFree text

Total Deducted and Balance Amount are maintained by payroll. Do not edit them by hand — that is how the same money gets recovered twice.

If the monthly amount will not clear the balance by the end month, the form warns you when you save.

Overtime Data

  1. New → Employee, Date, OT Total Hours
  2. Save — the rate and amount are calculated for you
  3. Set Overtime Status to Approved or Rejected, then Submit

Only submitted and approved overtime is paid. The Worked on Sunday tick is set automatically and earns the extra day separately.

06 Payroll Computation Preview

Ctrl + G → Payroll Computation Preview

This shows what payroll would produce for a month — gross, loss of pay, fines, penalties, overtime, advance, ESI, arrears and net — without creating or changing a single salary slip.

The banner at the top restates the rules currently in force, so the numbers are never read out of context.

Reading a row

The report reads left to right as a story: what the employee earns, how many days they worked, what was charged and why, and what is left. Every deduction can be traced back to the attendance behind it, which means a disputed figure can be answered from the screen rather than argued about.

Columns to check first

ColumnWhy
Penalty Days (Raw) vs Penalty DaysDifferent values mean a cap was applied
CappedTicked when the safety limits held something back
Excess DeductionMoney the rules asked for that the month could not pay
Net SalaryAny row at ₹0 needs a human decision
Chargeable CheckoutsMissing check-outs left after the free allowance

How to use it before a payroll run

  1. Pick the month and run it
  2. Sort by Net Salary, lowest first — investigate anything at or near zero
  3. Check Excess Deduction for anyone the safety limits caught
  4. Where a figure looks wrong, fix the attendance behind it rather than the setting
  5. Re-run, and only then process payroll

If a whole column of figures looks wrong, that is a settings problem. If one person's figure looks wrong, that is an attendance problem. Fixing a setting to solve one person's row will quietly change everybody else's.

07 Things to know

Automatic 23:00 check-out

A scheduled job stamps a check-out at 23:00 for anyone who checked in but never checked out. It exists to stop those employees being marked Absent, and it is still running.

Every automatic check-out is now tagged as automatic, so the reports and payroll can tell it apart from a real one. Before this was fixed, the Previous Day Checkout Not Marked report could never show anything at all — the evidence was erased each night before anyone saw it.

Mis-taps in the check-in app

A double tap on the check-in machine can create a day with a huge apparent lateness and almost no working hours. The Working Hours column on the late and early reports is there to catch these. Minimum Working Hours To Fine in settings stops them being fined automatically.

Shift grace changed from 60 to 15 minutes

Shifts previously allowed 60 minutes before marking anyone late. That is now 15, per the requirement. Historic attendance was not re-marked, so figures before the change reflect the old rule.

Total Days is now the full calendar month

30 or 31 days depending on the month, not working days. This matters when comparing to older manual calculations.

Unmarked days count as Absent

A blank cell on the monthly sheet is not neutral — payroll treats it as an absence. Check the monthly sheet for blanks before running payroll.

If something looks wrong

  1. Check the filters first — most “missing data” is a date range.
  2. Check the Working Hours column before acting on any late or early row.
  3. For a pay figure, open the Payroll Computation Preview and read the row across before changing anything.
  4. Never change a setting to fix one person's number.
